About the Project

Built for people who refuse to rent their AI.

MAi-RAG-PA started with a simple question: why should your most sensitive documents, questions, and thoughts have to leave your machine to get a useful answer?

The problem we couldn't ignore

For the last few years, "AI" has meant one thing: a browser tab pointed at someone else's server. Every prompt you type, every file you upload, every "remember this for me" — all of it goes to a data center you'll never see, owned by a company you don't control, governed by a policy you didn't write.

That works fine for casual questions. It works terribly for the things that actually matter:

  • Your legal contracts. Client confidentiality doesn't survive a cloud API call.
  • Your medical records. Your doctor's notes are not training data.
  • Your financial statements. Your tax strategy shouldn't be a marketing insight for someone else.
  • Your manuscript, your research, your private journal. These are things you wrote for you.

And beyond privacy, there's a quieter problem: you never actually own any of it. Stop paying the monthly fee, and your AI stops existing. Your "assistant" is a subscription, not a tool. You're renting a brain.

"A tool you can't take with you isn't a tool. It's a leash."

— The principle behind MAi-RAG-PA

So we built the opposite

MAi-RAG-PA is an AI framework that runs entirely on your hardware. Not "primarily local." Not "local-first, cloud fallback." Entirely local. The language models, the vector database, the memory, the conversations, the citations — all of it lives in a folder on your machine.

You install it with one command. You feed it your documents. You talk to it. It answers — and if you lose internet, nothing breaks. Because nothing was ever relying on the internet in the first place.

There's no account. No login. No telemetry. No upsell prompt in the middle of your work. There's no "Pro" tier that unlocks features you should have had from the start. It's just the tool, running for you, on your machine, for as long as you have the machine.

An honest boundary

Local AI is not a magic replacement for cloud AI. The biggest cloud models are still smarter at open-ended reasoning. They're faster on absurdly complex queries. They'll know more trivia than a 14B parameter model ever will.

But for the work that actually matters — reading your own documents, summarizing your own notes, helping with your own research, drafting your own writing — a locally-run model that reads your files is more useful than a frontier model that has never seen them, and never will.

What we believe

Four principles that shape every decision about the project.

🔒

Privacy is a right, not a feature

Not something you unlock with a premium plan. Not something you toggle on. The default. The only mode.

🏠

You own the tools you depend on

Software that stops working when you stop paying isn't software you own. It's software you're borrowing.

🎭

Specialists beat generalists

One AI trained to be "everything to everyone" is mediocre at all of it. Private, focused specialists do real work.

🔍

Honesty over hype

We tell you what local AI can't do. We cite our sources. We admit uncertainty. So should your tools.

How the project sustains itself

MAi-RAG-PA isn't a startup chasing a valuation. It's a project built to last, funded in a way that doesn't compromise the principles above. Here's exactly how that works.

The framework itself: free, forever

The core MAi-RAG-PA application — every feature you see on the homepage — is open source under its own license. You can read the code. You can modify it. You can fork it. You can run it on as many machines as you want. No license keys. No activation servers. No "contact sales" gate hidden behind the next button.

This isn't generosity for its own sake. It's insurance. If we disappeared tomorrow, the software would still run. That's what it means to actually own something.

Role Creation: the service that funds the framework

Building a good Role is genuinely hard. It requires dataset engineering, chunking strategy, prompt architecture, hallucination defense, and model matching. Most people try, get mediocre results, and assume the framework doesn't work. It does — the Role just needs to be built properly.

So we build them for you. Role Creation is a paid, one-time service: you hand us your documents, we hand you a working specialist. The revenue from this funds continued development of the free framework. It's a clean loop — people who want the shortcut pay for it; people who want to build their own get the tools to do it.

Digital products: guides for the self-starters

Alongside the framework and the service, we publish guides, playbooks, and kits for people who'd rather learn the craft than pay someone else to do it. These are written as if we're teaching a colleague — no fluff, no "10 AI secrets that will change your life," just the actual methods that work.

If those three things — free framework, paid service, guides — ever stop being enough to keep the lights on, we'll tell you. We won't quietly pivot to selling your data or bolting a subscription onto the core. That's the whole point.

Behind the project

MAi-RAG-PA is built and maintained by a small, independent team. Not a venture-backed company, not a well-funded lab — just people who got tired of the same tradeoff every cloud AI service was asking for: hand over your data, pay every month, or don't get to use AI at all.

That tradeoff felt dishonest. So we built an alternative. Everything you see on this site started as a personal itch: "I want to talk to my own documents without uploading them to a company I've never met."

The project grew from there — one feature at a time, one user complaint at a time, one "wouldn't it be nice if it also did X" at a time. The changelog is the honest record of that growth.

We don't pretend to be neutral. We have opinions:

  • Local AI is the right default for anything sensitive.
  • Cloud AI is fine — as long as it's a choice, not the only option.
  • Open source is the only honest business model for AI infrastructure.
  • If a company needs to look at your data to serve you well, it's not serving you.

If that resonates with you, you're in the right place. If it doesn't, we hope you at least find something useful in the code — that's why it's there.

Who this is for

MAi-RAG-PA isn't for everyone. It's for people who recognize themselves in one of these:

  • The freelancer who handles three clients' confidential documents every week and can't risk putting any of them in a cloud API.
  • The researcher who reads 200 papers a year and wants to synthesize them without uploading the PDFs to a "productivity" startup.
  • The small business owner who wants AI help with contracts, invoices, and customer questions — but not at the cost of a monthly subscription that doubles every year.
  • The parent who wants a patient math tutor available at 9pm on a Tuesday, using the actual curriculum their kid's school uses.
  • The writer who wants an assistant that remembers their voice, their current project, and their last three chapters — without that data becoming someone's training corpus.
  • The privacy-conscious professional who has read the terms of service and decided the tradeoff isn't worth it.

If none of these sound like you, MAi-RAG-PA probably isn't the right tool. And that's fine — the alternative is still there, and it's a real option. We just want it to be one option, not the only one.

What's next

The changelog is where the future lives. A short version:

  • More document formats, faster ingestion, cleaner metadata tagging.
  • Better Role template library so self-starters have a stronger starting point.
  • Deeper integration between Roles and the calendar/task system.
  • Continued work on hallucination defense — the hardest problem in all of AI.

If you want to influence what comes next, the issue tracker is open. Real feedback shapes the roadmap more than anything else.

The framework is free. The service is optional. The data never leaves your machine. That's the whole pitch — everything else is details.
