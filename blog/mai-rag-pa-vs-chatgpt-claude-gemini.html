MAi-RAG-P.A. vs. ChatGPT vs. Claude vs. Gemini: The Complete Comparison

Four AI tools. Four different design philosophies. One honest breakdown of where each one wins — including the categories where local AI loses. If you're trying to decide what to use for what, start here.

I run MAi-RAG-P.A., so I have an obvious bias. I also other LLM's regularly — because local AI is not the right tool for every job. This comparison is my honest attempt to sort out where each one fits.

If you want a summary: use all four for different things. Below is why.

The four tools, in one line each

  • MAi-RAG-P.A. — a local AI workspace for privacy-critical document work, running entirely on your hardware.
  • ChatGPT — OpenAI's cloud assistant. Best general-purpose reasoning and the most mature ecosystem.
  • Claude — Anthropic's cloud assistant. Best for long documents, careful writing, and nuance.
  • Gemini — Google's cloud assistant. Best for search-integrated and multimodal work.

Three of them are cloud services. One of them isn't. That single difference shapes everything else.

Privacy

Winner: MAi-RAG-P.A. Not close.

This is the dimension where local AI is categorically different, not just "better." With ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini, every message you type travels to a server. Some companies train on your data by default; others don't. All of them retain it subject to policies you can't audit.

With MAi-RAG-P.A., nothing leaves your machine. Not because a company promised — because there's literally nowhere for it to go. The system doesn't have an outbound connection.

For casual questions, this distinction doesn't matter. For anything confidential — contracts, medical records, client work, unpublished writing, personal journaling — it's the whole point.

Output quality

Winner: the cloud trio. Especially Claude and ChatGPT.

If you want the honest answer: frontier cloud models are smarter than anything that runs on a laptop. GPT-5, Claude 4.5, and Gemini 2.5 are in a different league from Qwen 2.5 7B or Llama 3.2 8B, which are the models most people actually run locally.

For open-ended reasoning, complex multi-step thinking, novel problem-solving, and creative writing that needs to be genuinely excellent — cloud wins. This isn't close either.

But here's the nuance: quality is task-dependent. If your task is "summarize this contract I just uploaded," a 7B local model is perfectly adequate — because the hard part of that task isn't intelligence, it's having the right documents. Cloud models can't help you with documents they don't have.

So the practical split is:

  • Generic reasoning and creativity: Cloud wins clearly.
  • Work grounded in your own documents: Local AI is often more useful, because it can actually access the material.

Speed

Winner: Cloud, when the network is good.

Cloud models respond in 1–3 seconds. Local models take 5–30 seconds depending on hardware and model size. That's the reality.

But there's an asterisk: cloud speed depends entirely on your internet. On a plane, in a hotel with bad Wi-Fi, in an area with unreliable connectivity, or during a cloud service outage — local works exactly as it always does.

For people with solid connections, cloud wins. For people without them, local is the only realistic option.

Cost

Winner: MAi-RAG-P.A., and it's a different shape of cost entirely.

  • ChatGPT Plus: $20/month = $240/year
  • Claude Pro: $20/month = $240/year
  • Gemini Advanced: $20/month = $240/year
  • MAi-RAG-P.A.: $0 after install. Forever.

Over five years, a single $20/month subscription totals $1,200. All three cloud services combined would be $3,600. That's real money — especially if you're a solo freelancer without a software budget.

Hidden cost caveat: local AI requires a computer that can run it. If you don't already have one with 16GB+ RAM, "free" becomes "buy a better laptop." But most people reading this already have the hardware.

Control

Winner: MAi-RAG-P.A. Absolutely.

Control means several things:

  • Version control. If a new model update makes things worse for you, you keep the old one. Cloud services force-migrate you to new versions whether you want them or not.
  • Feature control. Local AI is open source. If you want a feature, you add it. If you don't like a change, you fork it.
  • Availability. Cloud services deprecate models, change pricing, add features you didn't ask for, or shut down entirely. Local AI runs as long as your computer runs.
  • No policy changes. OpenAI changed their data training policy twice. Anthropic has updated their terms. Google has shuffled Gemini into and out of products. Local AI's terms don't change because there are no terms.

This is the dimension most people underrate until something they depend on gets taken away.

Document handling

Winner: split. Both have strengths.

Cloud AI has gotten better at document uploads. You can attach a PDF to ChatGPT or Claude and ask questions about it. For one-off documents, this works well.

But cloud AI has limits:

  • You can't practically maintain a searchable library of hundreds of documents.
  • Every document you upload is on someone else's server.
  • Uploads have size limits; long documents get truncated.
  • There's no per-client or per-project isolation.

MAi-RAG-P.A.'s RAG system handles:

  • Hundreds of documents per knowledge base, 17 file formats
  • Multiple isolated knowledge bases (per client, per topic, per Role)
  • Persistent libraries that grow over time
  • Answers with citations to specific document and page

For one-off document questions, cloud is fine. For ongoing document workflows — especially confidential ones — local AI is the only option that actually works.

Ecosystem and integrations

Winner: Cloud. ChatGPT especially.

Cloud AI services have app stores, browser extensions, mobile apps, integrations with productivity tools, custom GPTs, API access, and everything else that comes from being part of a large ecosystem.

MAi-RAG-P.A. is a local app. It doesn't integrate with your Google Calendar in the cloud — it has its own calendar. It doesn't have a mobile app (though you can access it from your phone over your local network). It doesn't have an app store.

If you value ecosystem depth, cloud wins. If you value self-containment, local wins. Different priorities, both valid.

Where each tool genuinely shines

Let me try to sort this cleanly:

Use ChatGPT when: you need general reasoning, brainstorming, coding help, or creative writing and the content isn't confidential.

Use Claude when: you're working with long-form writing, nuanced editorial feedback, or complex document analysis where quality matters more than privacy.

Use Gemini when: you need multimodal input, Google Search integration, or you're already deep in the Google ecosystem.

Use MAi-RAG-P.A. when: you're working with confidential documents, maintaining a private knowledge base, need per-client isolation, or want AI that works offline and has no subscription.

The honest recommendation

Stop trying to pick one. Use two or three. The hybrid approach is genuinely better than either option alone:

  1. Cloud AI for generic reasoning. Keep a $20/month subscription to ChatGPT or Claude for the tasks where quality matters most.
  2. Local AI for everything confidential. All your documents, all your private work, all your sensitive questions.
  3. Local AI as a fallback. When the internet is down or a cloud service is having issues, you're not stuck.
  4. Local AI for long-running projects. When you need a persistent, growing knowledge base over months or years, local is the only practical option.

This isn't a compromise. It's actually the correct architecture. Cloud AI has strengths local AI can't match. Local AI has properties cloud AI structurally can't offer. Using both is the honest answer.

"The question isn't 'which AI is best?' The question is 'which AI fits the work I'm doing right now?' Anyone who tells you there's one right answer is selling something."

If you only take one thing from this

Cloud AI and local AI solve different problems. Cloud AI is a smart assistant for general reasoning. Local AI is a smart assistant for your own documents.

If your work is mostly general reasoning and creativity, you don't need local AI. Keep using ChatGPT.

If your work involves confidential documents you'd rather not upload, local AI is the only category of tool that will actually let you use AI on them.

Both things are true. Neither cancels the other. Use what fits.
