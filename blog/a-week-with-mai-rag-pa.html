A Week With MAi-RAG-PA: What a Local AI Assistant Actually Looks Like

Most articles about local AI describe features. This one describes a week. Seven days of using MAi-RAG-PA for actual work — the moments it shined, the moments it didn't, and the honest verdict at the end.

I've been running MAi-RAG-PA on a ThinkPad with 32GB of RAM for a while now. But most of my coverage of it has been about what it is. This is about what it's actually like — a plain-English diary of seven days.

I picked a normal week. Not a light week, not a heavy week. Just a Tuesday through Monday that looked like most others.

Monday — the contract that usually eats an hour

A new client sends a 32-page master services agreement. Standard at my consultancy. What I usually do: skim it, highlight the important bits, flag three things to ask about, send it back. Takes about 75 minutes.

This time I dropped it into MAi-RAG-PA and asked four questions:

  • "Summarize the payment terms, including invoicing schedule."
  • "List any clauses that limit my liability or scope of work."
  • "Does this contract have a non-compete or exclusivity clause?"
  • "What are the termination conditions for both parties?"

The whole thing took about six minutes. The AI cited the exact clauses and page numbers. I verified three of the citations by hand because I'm paranoid about this stuff — they were all accurate.

Time saved: roughly an hour. But more importantly, nothing was uploaded anywhere. This contract contains confidential client terms, and there's no way I'd have done this with ChatGPT.

Tuesday — the medical records that finally make sense

I've been collecting lab reports and doctor's notes in a folder for years. Hundreds of pages. Every time I want to check something specific — when I was last prescribed X, what my bloodwork trend has been — I flip through PDFs like it's 1998.

I ingested the whole folder into a "Personal Health" knowledge base. Took about 20 minutes to process.

Then I asked: "Show me my blood pressure readings over the last three years, and note any that were flagged by the doctor as high."

It came back with a table — 14 readings, dates, values, and citations to specific reports. Two were flagged in the original notes, and it surfaced both.

I'd been meaning to do this manually for about a year. It took me six minutes.

The thing that matters: this is the exact category of data I would never want in a cloud AI. Health records are about as personal as it gets. The fact that I can get this benefit without uploading anything is the reason I use local AI at all.

Wednesday — drafting a client proposal in someone else's voice

A returning client asked me to write a proposal for a project similar to one we'd done two years ago. They wanted the same tone, the same structure, the same level of detail.

I created a Role called "Proposal Writer for [Client]" with its own knowledge base containing that client's previous proposals and briefs. Wrote a system prompt describing the client's preferred tone and my standard structure.

Then I asked: "Draft a proposal for a project similar to the one in the 2024 folder, but focused on the new data integration work."

The first draft was about 80% of the way there. I spent 30 minutes editing rather than three hours writing from scratch. It wasn't magic — the AI didn't nail every detail — but it started me from a much better place.

What struck me: the output matched the client's voice because the Role had access to their past material. A generic AI would have written something that sounded like a generic proposal. This sounded like this client's proposals. That's a real difference.

Thursday — the calendar thing I didn't expect to like

I'll be honest: I didn't care much about the calendar feature when I first installed MAi-RAG-PA. I already use a calendar app. Why would I want another?

But the integration is subtle in a good way. I can ask the AI things like "What do I have coming up in the next two weeks that involves traveling?" and it reads from the calendar. Or "Move my Wednesday 3pm to Thursday morning" — and it just does it.

It's not a reason to install the software. But it's the kind of small friction-removal that adds up. I stopped opening a separate calendar app as often.

Friday — the moment I reached for cloud AI instead

I want to be honest about this. On Friday, I needed help brainstorming a complex strategic question — a genuine "think through this from scratch" kind of task, no documents involved, no confidentiality issue.

I opened Claude instead of MAi-RAG-PA, and I was right to. The cloud model was meaningfully better for open-ended strategic reasoning. My local 7B model gave me a reasonable but not excellent answer. The cloud model gave me a genuinely insightful one.

This is the honest boundary. Local AI is not a replacement for cloud AI at the frontier. For confidential document work, local wins by default. For "think out loud with me about something hard," cloud is still better.

Using both is fine. That's what I do. The mistake is pretending one replaces the other.

Saturday — getting my personal research library back

I've been saving PDFs of interesting papers for years. Maybe 300 by now. I have no idea what's actually in most of them.

I ingested the whole folder into a "Research" knowledge base. Processing took about 40 minutes.

Then I started asking questions:

  • "Which of these papers discuss the relationship between X and Y?"
  • "Summarize the main disagreements in the literature."
  • "Which papers cite the 2019 study on Z?"

It found things I'd forgotten I had. Three papers on a topic I'd been meaning to research for months. A citation I'd been trying to track down. A contradiction between two studies I hadn't noticed.

This is where local AI feels genuinely magical. Not because the AI is smart — because it read everything I'd been ignoring and made it searchable. The papers never left my machine, which matters because some of them are from paywalled journals I'd rather not see re-distributed.

Sunday — the quiet benefit I didn't notice until I thought about it

Sundays are personal admin days for me. Bills, insurance paperwork, family stuff. I used to spend an hour or two on this and feel like I'd barely made a dent.

This Sunday, I asked MAi-RAG-PA five questions about insurance coverage, pulled up my mortgage terms, and checked whether my auto-renewal on a subscription was coming up. Total time: 18 minutes.

The quiet benefit here is that I actually used the tools. With ChatGPT, I would have thought "well, I could ask about this, but uploading my insurance policy feels weird" — and then not asked. The privacy was the enabler. Without it, I just wouldn't have done the work.

Monday morning — what I noticed about the whole week

Looking back at the seven days, three things stand out:

1. The wins were quiet. No single moment felt like a revolution. It was just... less friction. Less time wasted. Less "I'll deal with that later." The value was in what didn't happen — no confidential documents uploaded, no subscriptions racked up, no accounts created, no data shared.

2. The documents were the point. Everywhere it genuinely helped, it was because I'd given it my files. Contracts, medical records, proposals, papers. The AI isn't smarter than the cloud models. It just had access to content that cloud models never would.

3. It didn't replace everything. I still used Claude twice that week for reasoning-heavy tasks. I still used a real calendar app for scheduling. I still used email for email. MAi-RAG-PA was an addition to my toolkit, not a replacement for all of it.

Who this week is representative of

If you recognize yourself in any of these, this is roughly the experience you'd have:

  • You deal with confidential documents regularly
  • You have a growing pile of PDFs you never look at
  • You'd like AI help but feel uneasy about uploading stuff
  • You own a laptop with 16GB+ RAM
  • You're willing to spend 20 minutes on setup

If instead you're someone who mostly asks generic questions and doesn't handle sensitive documents, the value is smaller. You'd probably be fine with ChatGPT. That's a legitimate choice.

The honest verdict after a week

MAi-RAG-PA is not for everyone. It's not a ChatGPT killer. It's not going to make you smarter or more productive in the way its marketing might imply.

What it is: a tool that makes your existing documents useful — without sending them anywhere. If that matches a problem you have, it solves it well. If it doesn't, you don't need it.

For me, the week confirmed what I'd suspected. Local AI isn't better than cloud AI. It's different — and for a certain category of work, it's the only option that's actually honest about what it's asking you to give up.

That's enough reason to keep using it.
